The Greek word **μεθοδεία** (transliterated _methodía_) comes from **μέθοδος** (_methodos_, "method, way, plan") and literally means **"a scheme, plan, or craftiness carried out with method"**.

It is often used in a **negative sense**, referring to **cunning strategies, deceit, trickery, or crafty devices**. For example, in the New Testament (Ephesians 4:14; 6:11), it is used to describe the **“schemes” or “wiles” of the devil**—systematic, crafty, deceptive methods.

So depending on context, it can mean:

- **Methodical plan or strategy**
    
- **Deceptive scheme, trickery, craftiness**

### Examples from Ancient Papyri (Non-Biblical Usage)

According to Liddell-Scott-Jones and several lexicons, the noun **μεθοδεία** also appears in late papyrus documents—not in the New Testament—with a more neutral meaning related to method or procedure, particularly in administrative or fiscal contexts. For instance:

> In a tax-related papyrus dated c. A.D. 421 (P.Oxy. VIII 1134.9), the phrase **"πρὸς τὴν μεθοδίαν ἀκολουθῶς τῷ δοθέντι"** is used, meaning roughly _“in a method corresponding to the account given by you”_ ([StudyLight.org](https://www.studylight.org/lexicons/eng/greek/3180.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Strong's #3180 - μεθοδεία - Old & New Testament Greek ...")).

Another papyrus from the sixth century (P.Oxy. I 136.18) uses **"τὴν μεθοδίαν τρέψαι"**, i.e., _“to conduct one’s dealings according to the method”_ ([StudyLight.org](https://www.studylight.org/lexicons/eng/greek/3180.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Strong's #3180 - μεθοδεία - Old & New Testament Greek ...")).

And one more says **"ἣν (...) μεθοδίαν περὶ τὴν εἴσπραξιν"**—meaning _“the method of collection adopted by me”_ ([StudyLight.org](https://www.studylight.org/lexicons/eng/greek/3180.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Strong's #3180 - μεθοδεία - Old & New Testament Greek ...")).

These examples clearly illustrate that in some secular or administrative texts, **μεθοδεία** simply means **“method,” “procedure,”** or **“the manner in which something is done,”** rather than any sense of deceit or craftiness.

The Greek **μεθοδεία** (_methodía_) comes from **μέθοδος** (_methodos_ = “pursuit, way of inquiry, method”) + the suffix **-εία** (used to form abstract nouns).

From this root, the main **English descendant** is:

- **Method** → directly from Latin _methodus_, borrowed from Greek _μέθοδος_.
- **Methodical** → “done according to a plan or system.”
- **Methodology** → “the study of methods.”

---

**Difference in meaning**:

- In everyday **English** today, "method" and its derivatives are neutral or positive (a systematic way of doing something).
    
- In **Koine Greek**, **μεθοδεία** often took on a negative nuance — “crafty scheme” or “trickery carried out with method” (as in Ephesians 6:11).
    
- In **secular papyri**, though, it kept the neutral sense of “procedure” or “system.”
    

So, in short: our modern words **method, methodical, methodology** are descendants of the same Greek root, though they lost the “scheming” nuance that the New Testament usage emphasized.
