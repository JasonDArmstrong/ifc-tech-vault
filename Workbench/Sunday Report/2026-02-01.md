#### Sermon Notes
Psalm of the week: Psalm 143. David says how he trusts God as a sheep trust a shepherd. He calls out for mercy for his situation ("I feel like I am sitting in darkness") and also for himself and his sins. He expresses his need for God and also how he craves God and his overwhelming mercy. David also clearly wants to learn. A teachable spirit is rare in the midst trouble because of one's urgency and anxiety to get out of their situation
Sermon: (14) Am I Elect? Scripture lesson: 2 Peter 1:1-15
People are saved because God chose them from eternity without any consideration of their works. It is not a bare fact, like 1+1=2 however. People find this truth troubling. How does one live positively in light of this truth? How can we be comforted not knowing if we are written in the book of life? 

1. Cautions regarding predestination: two errors regarding predestination.
	1. Do not fret over the number of the elect. The Bible mentions the small number of the elect. We might wrongly assume that God is stingy with his grace. We ought not think "if few are saved, why should I suspect he saves me?" That is not the way scripture presents election. think of the letters to the new Testament letters. They are written with great hope, calling all of the Philippian church members partners. Paul is not confirming all of the members' elections, but he is expressing his confidence in God's grace that he has hope for each one. And we should likewise see all with hope and not deep some reprobates. 
	2. Do not view election fatalistically. Some people reason that they may be reprobate; not chosen. the secret things belong to the Lord. We do know that those who reject Christ are lost, and it is not based on if we think we are lost. The Canons of Dort summarizes election and includes that we must study it without assuming the ways of the Most High. God gives reasons that we may be hopeful for our election. 

2. What comfort do we have?
	1. God makes sure promises. they are always and they are certain. God desires all to be saved and he says that if we ask for his grace we will HAVE IT! If we ask we will have it. Not if we ask and we are elected maybe possibly we will have it, if we ask we will have it. It's not a call for some people to ask, a nationality, a background, if anyone asks, they will have his grace. Knowledge of election is also not a requirement. You need to know your sin and his sufficiency.
	2. God teaches us to call him father. How much does the father love the son? He calls him "with whom I am well pleased." When Jesus is asked "Lord, how should we pray?" he doesn't say begin by saying "the Father," he says say "our Father."
	3. He gives us sacraments that confirm our faith. The signs and seals of the covenant confirm and assure those who fear him. Assures them that he is close to those who fear the Lord. John is given a vision of heaven and what does he see? He says behold I looked and I saw a great multitude no man could number. All nations, tribes, peoples, and tongues.
