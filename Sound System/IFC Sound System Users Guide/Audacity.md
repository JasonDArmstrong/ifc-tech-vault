# **Job Aid: Recording Sermons with Audacity**

## **Introduction**

This job aid explains how to use **Audacity** to record and manage sermons. It is written for volunteers and staff who may not have prior audio experience. The goal is to make recording simple, reliable, and consistent each week. Follow the steps below to start, stop, save, and label sermon recordings.

---

### **1. Getting Started**

- Open Audacity
- Check audio devices (input = sound board/mic, output = headphones)
- Confirm input levels (not too quiet, not clipping)
### **2. Understanding the Interface**

- **1. Transport Controls** – Play, Stop, Record
- **2. Tool Toolbar** – Select and adjust clips
- **3. Level Meters** – Watch sound levels while recording
- **4. Device Toolbar** – Select mic/line input and playback device
- **5. Track Control Panel** – Mute or solo a track
- **6. Track Gain Slider** – Adjust volume of a track
- **7. Waveform Display** – Shows the audio you are recording

### **3. Recording a Sermon**

- Press the **Record** button before the sermon starts
- Monitor sound levels (stay in the green, avoid red peaks)
- Press **Stop** at the end

### **4. Reviewing the Recording**

- Use **Play** to listen back
- Trim silence at the beginning or end if needed
- Save changes

### **5. Saving and Exporting**

- Save the **project file** (for editing later)
- Export the **audio file** (MP3 or WAV) for uploading/sharing
- Use a consistent file naming convention (e.g., _2025-09-07_Sermon_Title.mp3_)

### **6. Backing Up**

- Store a copy on the church’s designated backup drive or cloud storage

### **7. Troubleshooting Quick Tips**

- No sound? Check input device and cables
- Levels too high? Lower gain in track panel or sound board
- File too large? Export as MP3 instead of WAV

### Audacity Interface
![[Pasted image 20250907095626.png]]
### **1. Transport Controls**
The basic playback and recording buttons: Play, Pause, Stop, Skip to Start/End, and Record. These control audio playback and recording.
### **2. Tool Toolbar**
Selection, Envelope, Draw, Zoom, Time Shift, and Multi-tool. These tools let you select, move, zoom, or adjust audio clips.
### **3. Recording/Playback Level Meters**
Visual meters that show input (microphone) and output (playback) levels. Use them to check volume and prevent clipping.
### **4. Device Toolbar**
Lets you select the **audio host** (e.g., Windows WASAPI), **input device** (microphone/line-in), number of recording channels (mono/stereo), and **output device** (speakers/headphones).
### **5. Track Control Panel**
Located at the left of each track. Provides buttons for **Mute** and **Solo**, plus dropdowns for track effects and settings.
### **6. Waveform Display (Track Area)**
Shows the audio waveform. This is where you edit, cut, copy, paste, delete, and apply effects. The horizontal axis is time, vertical axis is amplitude.
